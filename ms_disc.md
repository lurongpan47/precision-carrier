# Discussion

Using a classical contemplative account as a list of constraints, I built a hierarchical active-inference model in which precision gates content. Graded dissolution follows from withdrawing precision, with the order fixed by one computable threshold. Because levels were labelled in the traditional order and $J$ rises along that labelling, agreement with the classical sequence is not independent evidence; the result is that one threshold predicts the order. Re-emergence runs as a coupled front with persistent mean-field hysteresis. Under stated assumptions, a closed-form condition is necessary for precision to rebound after the last collapse and, at larger residuals, sufficient in practice. Readiness in flat states is not determined by activity, so contentless states are not all alike, although the separation from deep sleep rests largely on an assumed precision. The model fails, or is silent, on several points: responsiveness does not recover on the failing-support path, and the order of content loss in meditation and the pattern of anaesthetic disruption are not explained.

## Relation to current theories

The model can be read as a minimal quantitative instance of the claims in the Beautiful Loop theory [15]. A maximally simple reality model corresponds to flat beliefs. High epistemic depth has no single counterpart, but the closed-loop gain $M$ and readiness $R$ measure how far a perturbation is shared through the hierarchy. The correspondence is partial. The model has no explicit self-model, and the prior precision $J_s$ is set from outside, not selected as a mental action as in Sandved-Smith et al. [14]. Formalising this control is the most direct extension. The fit should also not be over-read: the Beautiful Loop theory was developed partly to account for meditative states, and one of its authors is a long-term practitioner [15,16], so agreement with a contemplative source is not independent evidence for it.

The model and IIT are both compatible with the empirical point that a nearly silent substrate need not be unconscious [3,4]. They differ in what they take to explain it: IIT identifies experience with a cause–effect structure, whereas here the separating variable is an inferred precision with a specific neuromodulatory interpretation [12,13]. Whether that difference is empirically separable is unclear: gain manipulations (P7) would also alter causal structure, and slowing near instability (P2b) is generic to bifurcations, not specific to this account. My integration measure is a proxy, so nothing here tests IIT's $\Phi$ [4].

The first-order jumps resemble global ignition. But in global neuronal workspace theory, conscious access is tied to the broadcast of content [5], and a contentless state with high readiness has no obvious place in that account.

## What the tradition contributed, and where it fails

The tradition's process model was useful because it specifies sequence and because it separates two kinds of contentlessness, a distinction not made in most scientific taxonomies. Three disagreements should be stated plainly.

First, the classical sequence places hearing second [18,20], whereas auditory responses persist in unresponsive dying patients and their authors suggest hearing may be among the last senses to go, although they did not test other senses [35]. The mismatch negativity is automatic and need not imply hearing, and the authors do not claim awareness, but the P3b in some patients makes this reconciliation incomplete. The tradition's own practice of reading to the dying also presupposes a late-persisting channel, an internal tension I do not attempt to resolve. A larger $J$ at L2 would reproduce the data, but that would be post hoc.

Second, the anaesthesia and meditation data both resist a sensory-to-prefrontal reading of the hierarchy, and the model does not account for either. The contents of the three conceptual classes suggest a different reading: the 33 include fear, attachment, hunger and thirst, the 40 joy, amazement and generosity, the 7 forgetfulness, mistaken perception, doubt and lassitude [22]. These are largely affective and visceral dispositions and not discursive reasoning, although the lists are mixed. As a hypothesis, the hierarchy might be better read as an axis from sensory periphery through visceromotor cortex to the thalamic and brainstem core, in line with embodied predictive coding [32] and with the subcortical activations seen at emergence [9]. Discursive thought in lateral cortex would have no dedicated level in this reading and could drop out early, but I have not modelled this. The cost is that the $J$ gradient I used comes from macaque neocortex [25], and I have no direct estimate of it along this axis.

Third, the evidence for this order in [3] comes from Zen Shikantaza states with decreased thoughts but preserved percepts (8 practitioners) and from meditators' reports that simple bodily sensations were last to disappear. The guru-yoga dissolving phase measured in that study used a no-report paradigm, with no reported sequence. None of these is the wind yoga of the completion stage. The conflict with the classical order is thus not a direct test of the tradition's claim about those practices. It does, however, suggest that "the order" may not be a property of the system independent of the route.

Some claims lie outside the model altogether. In the tradition, the subtlest mind does not depend on the brain and continues across lives; in Jonang thought, clear light is held to be an unconditioned reality. In the model, readiness vanishes with its substrate, as all functional variables must. The negative EEG findings in putative *thugs dam* [38] do not test these claims, since the claims do not rest on brain activity and recordings began at least 26 hours after death. Nor does the model address why any of this should be accompanied by experience. What it establishes is a structural correspondence, not a validation of the tradition or a reduction of it to precision control.

## Limitations

This is a seven-node mean-field model with no oscillations or subcortical anatomy. It cannot produce sleep slow waves or alpha, so it describes deep sleep poorly and does not model meditative cessation. The rebound depends on assumptions A1 and A2 (Methods), and a full variational treatment of a single temperature would change it on the metastable branches where collapses occur. Waking is saturated in this formulation, with commitment close to one, so waking and dreaming-like states are nearly identical; integration in these states is uninformative, and the model does not reproduce the high perturbational complexity of wakefulness [30,39]. Quantitative comparison is therefore limited to contentless states. The deep-sleep-like state is defined by an assumed precision. Apart from the hierarchical gradient and the order of magnitude of the time constants, parameters are plausible values, not fitted ones. The sweep shows that the threshold results are not artefacts of particular values, not that those values are correct, and in a large fraction of sets return did not complete. The treatment of 33, 40 and 7 as alphabet sizes is heuristic. Path length is uncalibrated. The mapping of levels to classical stages is an input. Finally, the classical material is taken from the cited translations and secondary accounts, and traditions differ on details such as when the eighty conceptions cease [18]; this is not a text-critical study.

## Conclusion

Treating precision as the variable that gates content yields explicit dynamics for the dissolution and return of experience, a closed-form condition on when precision can rise after content is lost, and a readiness variable that can differ between contentless states. The account makes predictions that existing data do not settle, and it disagrees with the classical source, and fails to explain some of the data, in ways that are stated. Contemplative process models may be most useful to neuroscience in this way, as precise claims about sequence and state that models can be built to meet and data can refute.
